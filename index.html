About this mandala

The purpose

This mandala brings three maps of the inner life into one picture: the nervous system, from polyvagal theory (where is my body?); the parts and the Self, from Internal Family Systems (who is here?); and awareness (how do I notice, and how do I come back?). Used together, they offer a simple way through: notice where you are, name it, and return, letting the Self lead. Awareness lets you know when you are at the edge and bring yourself back, and each return builds on the last, so over time the window widens. The aim is not to be rid of what lies outside the window, which is part of being human, but to find the way back more easily and more quickly. Beneath all three is one aim: to cultivate a true sense of Self beyond conditioning, the programs and imprints laid down by early experience that can keep a life small long after they were needed. And all of it moves toward coherence: heart and brain in rhythm, harmony within the nervous system, and the parts working together, with the Self leading.

A model, made from models

How it is built

At the center is the Self, in two layers: the Flower of Life at its heart, and around it eight petals, one for each of its qualities. Around them is the window of capacity, the range in which you can feel strong things and stay connected, divided into four directions. Inside the window, each direction is a healthy state: energized, still, settled, and connected. Just outside the window's dashed edge are the early signals of each: revving, bracing, sinking, and over-tuning. Outside those are the four defenses: fight or flight, freeze, collapse, and fawn, which flow into one another as one continuous ring. At each junction between two defenses sit two small arrows, one in each direction, for the movement between them, and four gold doorways on the window's edge mark orienting, the way back in.

The window is not fixed. Drag "Your window" and the center grows while the threat rings thin, the way practice, support, and safety can widen it over time. The braid at the edge weaves the three nervous systems together: gold for ventral, the safe and connected state; red for sympathetic, fight or flight; and blue for dorsal, collapse.

An evolutionary view

Each state on this wheel is an inheritance. Collapse is the oldest, shared with reptiles and fish: playing dead. Fight and flight belong to every mammal. The social, connected state, and the ability to calm one another with a face and a voice, is the newest. Polyvagal theory orders them this way; some physiologists dispute its anatomy, but the picture of defenses as survival strategies, each wise in its time, is widely shared. Nothing on the outer ring is a flaw. Each is the body trying to keep you alive.

Going gently

Reading this map can itself move you toward an edge. Notice, and come home whenever you need to: the button at the top is always there. If you find yourself outside your window often, or for long, or if what surfaces is trauma, a trauma-informed therapist can help widen your window in a way a map cannot. If you are in crisis, contact your local emergency number, or in the United States call or text 988.
