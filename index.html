About this mandala

The purpose

To bring polyvagal theory, the window of capacity, and the heart and brain science of neurovisceral integration and coherence into one picture of the nervous system: notice where you are, name it, and return toward the center. The fuller purpose is at the top of How to read.

A model, made from models

How it is built

How to read names each ring. Two dials turn. "Lately" is your window over weeks and months: toward the top, coherence spreads and the window widens while the ring of survival thins and lets the light through; toward the bottom, the ring thickens into a dense shell and the window narrows. "Right now" is where you are this moment: the rings hold their size, and you follow the way out through the alarm into survival, and the way back across the edge and home. And the braid round the edge weaves the three nervous systems together: gold for ventral, red for sympathetic, and blue for dorsal.

An evolutionary view

Each state on this wheel is an inheritance. Collapse is the oldest, shared with reptiles and fish: playing dead. Fight and flight belong to every mammal. The social, connected state, and the ability to calm one another with a face and a voice, is the newest. Polyvagal theory orders them this way; some physiologists dispute its anatomy and its evolutionary story, but the picture of defenses as survival strategies, each wise in its time, is widely shared. Nothing on the outer ring is a flaw. Each is the body trying to keep you alive.

An injury, not an illness

Trauma can come from too much, too soon, or too fast, or from too little of what was needed, for too long, without enough time, space, permission, protection, or resources for the body to come back. A survival response gets stuck. That makes it an injury, not an illness. Trauma-informed care asks "What happened to you?" rather than "What is wrong with you?", and a wider lens adds: what happened to your people, and what is still happening (adapted from Linda Thai).

Going gently

Reading this map can itself move you toward an edge. Notice, and come home whenever you need to: the button at the top is always there. If you find yourself outside your window often, or for long, or if what surfaces is trauma, a trauma-informed therapist can help widen your window in a way a map cannot. And safety is not only an inner state. For people living with danger, discrimination, or oppression, the body may be reading the danger accurately, and its alarms and shutdowns are skills that have kept them alive. Coming home here is not about calm as control, or learning to feel safe where it is unsafe. It is about building capacity, together, and making room for rest and joy even in hard times. Healing moves at the speed of trust. If you are in crisis, contact your local emergency number, or in the United States call or text 988.
