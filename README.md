# The Window Mandala

A map of the window of capacity, with the Self at its center: the four directions a nervous system moves under threat, the movements between them, and the way home, with practices for widening the window.

A companion to [The Return Mandala](https://erinsarita.github.io/return-mandala/) and [The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala](https://erinsarita.github.io/prenatal-mandala/). Created by Erin Singleton, a student in APPPAH's Prenatal & Perinatal Educator certification. A model for reflection and education, not diagnosis, therapy, or medical advice.

## The purpose

Three maps of the inner life, brought into one picture, each answering a question:

- **Where is my body?** Polyvagal theory: the ventral window, sympathetic fight or flight, dorsal collapse, and the blends between. And the neurovisceral integration model (Julian Thayer and Richard Lane, 2000, 2009): the loop between the prefrontal cortex, the amygdala, and the heart (through the vagus nerve) that holds the window, strains at the edge, and breaks outside it. Leaving the window can go two ways: in fight or flight the brake gives way (under-regulation); in collapse it clamps down too hard, like braking until the engine stalls: the body shuts down and feelings go numb or far away (over-regulation). The wheel opens with the loop drawn around the Self and the window (heart, brainstem, the alarm at the edge, the brake, and the border of the window of capacity, each cut into the four directions with hover cards); "Show the simpler wheel" under the wheel switches it off.
- **Who is here?** Internal Family Systems: the parts that take over when we leave the window, and the Self at the center.
- **How do I notice, and how do I come back?** Awareness: noticing your own state as it happens, with attention and peripheral awareness. Awareness brings presence, presence helps you return toward the ventral center, and over time it widens the window.

Used together: notice where you are, name it, and return, letting the Self lead. Beneath all three is the deeper aim: to cultivate a true sense of Self beyond conditioning, the old programs and imprints from early experience that once protected us and now hold us back. Parts carry the imprints; the Self is not made of them.

Awareness lets you know when you are at the edge and bring yourself back; each return builds on the last, so the way home grows shorter and the window widens. The aim is not to be rid of what lies outside the window, which is part of being human, but to find the way back more easily and more quickly.

All of it moves toward coherence, which means three related things here: in the body, heart coherence (heart rhythm and breath in step, which can be measured); among the parts, harmony (parts trusting the Self enough to work together); and in the mind, clarity. They support one another, but only the first is measured by heart rate variability. The Self tab's "Coherence: harmony within" section spells this out. Practices opens with "Coherent breathing" at six breaths a minute, moving to the heart and to appreciation partway through, with a heart-focused step adapted from HeartMath, and a caution at the top: if slow breathing leaves you heavy, foggy, dizzy, or far away, stop, and start with movement or warmth instead.

On the wheel, the soft halo around everything is the field of peripheral awareness; the Awareness tab explains it, and the Practices tab adds "Opening peripheral awareness."

## The model

A synthesis of earlier models, described in our own words: the window of tolerance (Siegel), the ladder of states (Porges, Dana), the defense cascade (Schauer and Elbert), fawn (Walker), tend and befriend (Taylor and colleagues), the thaw and orienting (Levine), the Self and its parts (Schwartz, Internal Family Systems), and the science of resilience (Harvard's Center on the Developing Child). The circle, the four directions, the healthy root of each defense, and the transitions are this mandala's own synthesis, theory-based and offered from an evolutionary perspective.

- **The center:** the Self as a lotus, on the window's own color (it runs in under the petals, so no dark ring shows behind them): the Flower of Life drawn in gold at its heart, its central circle (the one holding the six-petaled flower) emphasized in light, a small dot of light at the very center for the witness, and one ring of eight large lotus petals around it, one for each quality of Self, reaching out over the inner edge of the window, with a soft yellow glow.
- **Inside the window:** one continuous soft-yellow ring that blends between four healthy states, one per direction: energized, still, settled, connected. Each is the regulated, safety-held form of the defense beyond it (Deb Dana's blended states). Where there is room, a small line under each names its blend: ventral + sympathetic, ventral holding both, ventral + dorsal, ventral, most social. Its edge is marked by a soft band of light, with four gold doorways for orienting.
- **At the edge:** the early signals of leaving the window: revving, bracing, sinking, over-tuning. In dark mode the edge takes a lighter tint of its defense's color.
- **Outside the window:** fight or flight (top), freeze (right), collapse (bottom), fawn (left), as one continuous ring: the colors flow into one another round the circle with no border between them.
- **The movements:** at each junction between two defenses, two slender tapered arrows, one in each direction (eight in all). The braid is a three-strand plait of the three nervous systems: gold for ventral (safe and connected), red for sympathetic (fight or flight), and blue for dorsal (collapse).
- **Look and feel:** a soft pastel palette on warm ivory (a deep plum night in dark mode, where the window stays yellow), Cormorant Garamond for the wheel and headings, and a halo of light round the ring. Hovering lights only the section under the pointer.
- **Your window:** a slider that widens the window around a fixed center: the Self stays the same size, the window grows around it, and the rings of threat thin. Distance from the center shows how much the Self is leading, not whether it is there. The "How wide is mine?" reflection sets it.
- **What's building:** a second slider for the load as it builds, with five paths to choose from: toward fight or flight, freeze, collapse, or fawn, or "If it keeps building" (the defense cascade). Each direction walks the same loop, from the window through its own edge signal (revving, bracing, sinking, over-tuning) to the brake, which strains, and meets the rim in its own way: the brake gives way in fight or flight (a crack in the rim), slams on while the body is still charged in freeze (a dark clamp over a red line of charge), clamps down until the body shuts down in collapse (a dark clamp, and the wheel dims), and keeps a friendly face over the fear in fawn (a pale, dashed mask). The captions use plain words and a car analogy, and marks under the slider show where each stage begins. One line under the slider names each stage, with a note that the direction depends on the situation and on what the body learned. The breakpoint sits at the window's border, so a wider window holds more before the rim is crossed.
- **Body view:** a toggle under the wheel ("Body view" / "Self view") that reads the same wheel through the body. At the center, heart-brain coherence, drawn as glowing rings around a white-gold heart, brightest at the center like the gold nimbus of an icon, with one label, "coherence, deepening toward the heart". The card's "As coherence deepens" section describes qualities that may arise as it deepens (receptive, attuned, absorbed, unity, and "beyond words"; `COH_LAYERS` in the script), with the evidence for each, a "not a ladder" note, and a go-gently note (Lindahl and colleagues, 2017). The window states are named as the body feels them (warm, strong, quick; steady, alert, watching; heavy, warm, digesting; soft face, warm voice), with soft chevrons on the diagonals showing that connection grows inward. The halo becomes "if it lasts", the long-term cost of each survival state (allostatic load, McEwen 1998; low HRV and heart disease risk, Thayer and colleagues 2010). Its card notes that the heart's electrical field is measurable, and that claims of it healing or syncing others are not well established. The Body view card also has a "Receptivity" section: in safety, attention widens and intuition can be heard (interoception and gut feelings, Dunn and colleagues 2010; Damasio 1994; insight and positive mood, Subramaniam and colleagues 2009), with synchronicity held as a felt experience rather than a claim (Jung). A "Receptive sensing" practice (eyes closed or blindfolded, safety first, with a trauma-aware caution) joins Practices, and the center reads "coherence: open, receptive". An "Intuition, or an old alarm?" section compares conditioned fear with intuition side by side, with a "settle, then ask again" check and the science (fear generalization, Dunsmoor and Paz 2015; mood coloring judgment, Schwarz and Clore 1983; when gut feelings can be trusted, Kahneman and Klein 2009), plus a note that fear is sometimes accurate. The Perception section in the Awareness tab carries a short version that links to it.
- **Perception:** each direction shows "How the world looks from here", inside the window and outside it (`PERCEIVE` in the script). The Awareness tab has a "Perception" section: the brain predicts what is out there and the state shapes the guesses (Barrett), threat narrows the lens and settled states broaden it (Arnsten; Fredrickson), self-fulfilling loops (Downey and colleagues), the first mirror of caregivers (Cooley; Madon and colleagues; Meins and colleagues; linking to The Return Mandala), before birth (Kisilevsky and colleagues; Glover; linking to The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala), and growth from hardship (Gross; Tedeschi and Calhoun). A caution note says perception does not cause what happens to people, and that quantum claims about shaping events are not supported.
- **Find on the map:** the wheel opens as a clean foundation (the center, the window, the loop rings, the edge, the brake and border, and the survival states). A "Find on the map" index under it (`FINDS` in the script) lights up where each idea usually lives, dims the rest, adds a short pin where useful, and opens its card: States (flow, monkey mind, coherence, receptivity, absorption, unity), The body (heart rate variability, the alarm, the brake, if it lasts), and The mind (parts, perception, intuition or an old alarm). Items that live in the Body view switch to it, and Parts switches back to the Self view. Tap a lit chip again to clear it. New ideas can be added as new entries.
- **Flow and monkey mind:** shown when chosen in the index. Flow is a translucent band all the way around the window (both views), widest on the Energized side where it is most common and best studied, quieter on Still (absorbed attention) and Connected (group flow), and thinnest toward Settled, where deeper rest becomes relaxation. In the Body view it laps over the outer layers of coherence: flow shares the receptive, attuned, and absorbed qualities, but usually not resting heart coherence, since it comes with effort. Monkey mind is its mirror, a restless broken line at the edge all the way around: racing when revving, scanning when bracing, ruminating when sinking, replaying when over-tuning. Each direction names its own form of both (`FLOW_DIR`, `MONKEY_DIR`). Their cards cover the research (Csikszentmihalyi 1990; Peifer and colleagues 2014; Keller and colleagues 2011; de Manzano and colleagues 2010; Dietrich 2004; Ulrich and colleagues 2014; Dixon and colleagues 2018; Killingsworth and Gilbert 2010; Brosschot, Gerin, and Thayer 2006; Baird and colleagues 2012).

The four directions are named for what the body does, so each name holds the healthy state and the defense: Moving (energized to fight or flight), Pausing (still to freeze), Resting (settled to collapse), and Reaching (connected to fawn). Each direction's panel walks its six layers from the center out (inside the window, the heart, the brainstem, the alarm at the edge, the brake, outside the window), with research for each (`PATHS` and `LAYERS` in the script). The outside layer carries a short evidence note on what is known about the heart there: well studied for fight or flight, mixed for freeze, less clear for collapse, not yet measured for fawn. Clicking any quarter of a loop ring opens that direction at that layer.

Tabs: How to read, Where am I?, The Self, Awareness, The four directions, Patterns (the routes, moving between, and triggers), and Practices (for inside the window: cultivating the Self and widening the window, plus building resilience).

## Patterns on the wheel

The Patterns tab draws routes across the mandala itself. Each pattern (fight and fawn, flight and freeze, freeze and collapse, fawn and collapse, straight to fight, a room never visited, rapid swings, hovering at the threshold, and coming home with practice) has a "Show on the wheel" button that draws its line, with a start dot and an arrow. A route is a list of [angle, depth] points mapped onto the rings as drawn, so it keeps its place as the window slider moves. "Show the parts map" places five example parts in their home states, with dashed unblending lines back to the Self. "Map your own pattern" lets a visitor tap the states they moved through and see their route; it is kept in memory only. Leaving the Patterns tab clears the wheel.

## My map

"Make my map" (under the slider) turns on map mode. Tapping the wheel places a marker where something tends to take you; a form asks what it is (a part of me: manager, firefighter, exile, or not sure; or in my life: person, place, thing, environment, or situation, each with its own white icon on a disc in its own color from the mandala palette), whether it is a glimmer (gold spark badge) or a trigger (red bolt badge), which starts out matching where it sits, a name, an optional note, and where it sits (a list of regions, which also lets you add without tapping). While mapping, and on the printout, the rings' smaller lines of text step aside to make room for names, and each name is placed clear of the ring names and arrows, wrapping long names in full. A key of the icons and badges appears in the panel, on the print, and on the download. Markers can be dragged, or tapped to edit or remove. The My map panel lists everything by region: with the Self, inside the window (what steadies you), at the edge (early signals), and outside (what takes you into each defense). "Print my wheel" prints a two-page sheet (the wheel, then the legend) in the light theme; "Download my wheel" makes a letter-proportioned PNG at print resolution with the wheel, the title and date, and a two-column legend, with the display font embedded. The map is not saved: it lives in memory and is gone when the page is closed or reloaded.

## The file

The mandala is the single file `index.html`. The content lives in constants near the top of the script: `DIRS` for the four directions and their three layers, `MOVES` and `CROSSINGS` for the transitions, `FEEL` for the emotions that tend to arise in each part of each direction (shown in its panel), `PATTERNS` (with each route's trace, and the parts map), `HOME`, `SELF`, `PATTERNS`, `REG`, `PRACTICES`, `WIDTH`, `FINDER`, and `MODEL_NOTE`. Nothing a visitor answers or maps is saved; only the light or dark choice is kept, under the `wm-` key.

## The center, in layers

The Self tab describes the center in four layers, each with a practice: the witness, a small dot of light at the very center (the one who notices, the still point; "Resting as awareness"), the Flower of Life (the Self; "A meditation into the Self"), the petals (the qualities, Self-present; "Meeting a part: the six Fs"), and the window (Self-led; "A parts check-in"). Hovering a layer lights it on the wheel, and the center has its own hover card. The stages of returning run in and back out: notice, unblend, Self-present, rest, Self-led. The Awareness tab adds how a wider awareness widens the window and deepens the Self, toward samadhi.

## Where am I? The way back that fits

The finder (three questions) or a row of choices (fight or flight, freeze, collapse, fawn, at the edge, inside the window) opens the way back for that state: how to recognize it, what it needs, practices with why each one fits that state, what can wait ("Not yet"), and what comes next. Each state needs something different: fight or flight moves the energy before slowing it (stillness such as yoga nidra can feel like a trap), freeze starts with tiny movements, collapse warms and gently activates rather than calming further, fawn turns attention back to oneself. The content is `WAYS` in the script; each direction's panel summarizes its way back and links here. Inside the window, the Practices tab holds the still, inward practices. There are no animated or audio-guided practices; all practices are written.

The co-regulation note in Coming home links to The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala, which follows co-regulation from the very beginning.

**Adapted from Linda Thai's course, Stuck in Survival: Somatic Experiments Towards Embodied Safeness.** Each state's "Where am I?" card and direction card now name the story it tells (state creates story, Deb Dana, as mapped by Janina Fisher and Linda Thai: connection, protection, protection held still, disconnection, fear-based attachment; `STORY`), and a "Completing the stress response" set of practices for each direction (`STRESS`): run in the chair, growl and push, chew, crunch, carry; the slow turtle, push the walls away, rock and sway; come to slowly, pat the edges, arms and heels; reach, grasp, pull, have, let yourself be held, hold yourself. Practices gains Let it land (somatic stitching), Awe and wonder, and From fright to delight. The Awareness tab's Perception section explains state creates story.

Stage 2 from the same course: "Find on the map" gains **The early alarm** (alert, orient, find, fidget; safety and safeness; what tips us toward the edge) and **At the rim** (the vagal brake lifting, the stress axis, the thinking brain and Broca's area going quiet, the body's own painkillers in shutdown; Rauch and colleagues 1996). The edge's ways back add Ask, and find, and Fidget on purpose. Direction cards add two kinds of stillness (freeze braced, collapse limp), the attachment cry before fawn, and fawn or appease (after Nkem Ndefo) (`OUT_MORE`). Patterns adds Linda Thai's loops: stuck on, stuck off, and alive but not too much; rapid swings notes "ricocheting". The window's basics list its qualities and the social engagement system; How to read adds "An injury, not an illness" and a justice-aware note in Going gently; the Body view adds "What flexibility protects".

**The petals.** Beyond the survival ring, a lotus petal rises from behind the braid at each direction, holding the ways back from that state (`drawPetals`, `PETAL`). Tap one to open its way back. The frame widened from 490 to 562 to make room (`VB`; the glow keeps to the wheel, `WHEEL`).

Stage 3 from Linda Thai's course: **My resources map**, after her Regulating Resources Map (`RES_ZONES`, `showResources`). Five zones (staying in my window, and coming back from fight or flight, freeze, collapse, and fawn; her map has three, and this wheel gives fawn its own), each with things I can do on my own and with others, tap-to-add ideas, and her wider view of attachment (land, ancestors, culture, body, deep time; `RES_ANCHORS`). What helps you stay is written inside the window; what brings you back fills each direction's petal, your own things in dark type and things with others in rose. Print or download it as an image; on the printout an empty petal is left blank to write in. Like My map, nothing is saved. Reached from the "My resources" button under the wheel, Practices, and each way back.

**Coherence moves with the window.** The "Your window" slider now widens a field of coherence too (`cohEdge`): the Self and its petals stay fixed, coherence spreads out from them into the window, the window grows around it, and the ring of survival thins. On the regular wheel it shows as a soft white-gold light with a dotted gold edge and the word "coherence"; in the Body view the layers of coherence spread with it, under the window's names, and the Coherence chip in Find on the map follows.

**Building coherence.** Practices opens with a set of four (`COH_BUILD`): heart-focused breathing, humming or chanting (Bernardi and colleagues 2001), rhythmic movement (Perry 2009), and co-regulation (Feldman and colleagues 2011; Vickhoff and colleagues 2013), with Coherent breathing as their base. It is linked from the coherence cards in the Self tab and the Body view, and from "Building coherence" under the window slider.

**Three circles at the Body view's center.** The heart alone gave way to three overlapping circles, mind, heart, and body with its nervous system, each ringed faintly for its own electrical rhythm, brightest where all three overlap. "Coherence" and "deepening toward the center" sit close around them. The coherence card explains the circles and notes what is measured (brain, heart, and breath falling into step; the heart's field measurable a short way from the body) and what is early (claims that the field carries feeling between people).

**The window's glow takes in the alarm.** In the simpler wheel, the alarm now runs straight on from the window's steady center, and the glowing ring at the window's edge widens out over it, brightest at its outer edge, where the window ends and the orienting doorways sit (`winglow`, `WB`). So revving, bracing, sinking, and over-tuning read as inside the window, as the loop view and How to read say. It follows Linda Thai's window, adapted from Dan Siegel, which places a band of too much or too little energy between the window's steady center and survival.

The three circles now glow: white light with a fine gold outline, each radiating a warm gold halo, inside a bloom of light that fades out past their edges, against a coherence area of soft, light gold. In the Body view the Self's cream ground stays for taps but turns transparent, so the layers of coherence show right to the center.

**Two dials, side by side.** "Lately" (your window) and "Right now" (where you are) replace the window slider and the "What's building" slider. Lately is capacity over weeks and months: the height of the knob sets the window (`capOf`, `setCap`); up the right side resonance grows, down the left protection builds; as the window narrows the ring of survival darkens into a dense shell, and as it widens rays of light shine out through it; a small sphere in the dial echoes it. Right now is state: the rings keep their size, and clockwise from the top you pass through the window, the alarm, and survival (driving the old `drawArousal` drawing, with its five direction chips), then the way back across the edge, which can feel like alarm again (shaking, heat, tears, a rush of energy), and back into the window and home (`renderNow`, `drawBack`). The Lately dial sets how much of the Right now dial the window takes, so a wider window gives more room before the alarm.

**The nervous system, and its rhythms.** The mandala is now one picture of the nervous system: the Body view is the only wheel, with the three circles of mind, heart, and body at the center. The Self (the petals and Flower of Life, The Self tab, the Internal Family Systems lens, and the parts in the directions, Patterns, Practices, and My map) and the Awareness tab and lens were taken out, to be explored in mandalas of their own. Where it fits, the text says that inside the window of capacity the qualities many call Self come forward (calm, curiosity, compassion, clarity, confidence, courage, creativity, connection) and the connection with Self is easier to feel, and that coherence and resonance go with awareness and presence. The Perception section moved into the Body view, with a new paragraph: awareness depends on where you stand, narrowed and bent toward threat in survival, clear in resonance, where you can see things more as they are, make connections, and gain insight and wisdom. How to read, the purpose, the about text, and Into the center were rewritten to match.

**The simpler wheel by default, with the brake as one line.** The wheel now opens on the simpler view; "Show the loop" turns on the heart, brainstem, brake, and border rings. In the simpler wheel the brake is a single gold line just outside the window's glowing border, labeled on the diagonals, with its own hover card (`.brake-line`; `R.brake` and `R.rim` are that thin band in the simple geometry). The Right now dial strains, cracks, or clamps that line, and the alarm, brake, and rim chips in Find on the map now work in the simpler wheel; only Heart rate variability turns the loop on.

**The braid encircles everything, with golden rays.** The braid now runs round the whole mandala, outside the petals (`R.braid` 546 to 572); the petals rise from just beyond the "if it lasts" band, and the frame widened to 618 to fit. Outside the braid, dense golden rays show resonance, like the halo of light round a figure in an old painting (`drawRays`): one every 0.6 degrees, bright gold at the base and fading at the tips over a soft glow. They lengthen as the Lately dial turns toward the center; they stand tallest at the eight points where the wheel's lines cross, step down shorter on either side, rise a little at each midpoint, and taper again. A third model chip, **Coherence and resonance: mind, heart, and body**, lights the center and explains resonance frequency, coherence, and the heart's field, with what is measured and what is early (Vaschillo and colleagues 2006 added). The lenses now work on the simpler wheel, and the polyvagal lens also lights the petals.

**A quieter wheel.** The wheel keeps its names; the smaller lines (each state's words, the blends, the held feelings, "if it lasts", "the brake") step back to the cards and hover tips, and the petals show their words only on My resources map. The petals are a ring of twelve, edge to edge, drawn the way The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala draws its practice petals (`petalD`): from the ring, each edge swells gently out and meets the other in a soft point. The four at the directions stand a little larger and hold the ways back from each state; the two at each diagonal open the way back from each movement, one for each arrow. The Lately dial is gold across its top half, around "center", and purple across its bottom half, around "protection". The rays follow the Right now dial (`nowShine`): tallest and brightest at the center, easing through the window and the alarm, nearly out deep in survival, and coming back on the way home. The dials sit right under the wheel, and on wide screens the wheel is sized so both fit in view together.

The Right now dial is mirrored left and right (`nowZones`: `wEnd`, `aEnd`, `sEnd`, `rEnd`): the Lately dial widens or narrows the window on both sides at once, the way back (return) mirrors the alarm, and survival fills what is left at the bottom, so a wider window means more room before the alarm and an earlier return.
